In one breath

Your conversation, remembered.

MyScribe writes down what was actually said, so people can go back to it. You ask first, it listens, then it gives you three things:

A summary. The story of the conversation, in plain words, every sentence linked to the moment it was said.

Ask Your Conversation. Ask "was it one tablet or two?" and it shows you the exact words. It never makes up an answer.

Share. Send the summary to whoever you choose. They don't need the app to read it.

The framing

The AI surge is real. We're the gentle end of it.

The language around MyScribe matters more than most products, because the whole thing is about words: what was actually said, and nothing added. A single word can tip it from reassuring to alarming.

The surge is real. Every clinician is about to get an AI scribe, and that's the moment we ride. But MyScribe isn't a shield against clinicians or against AI, so words like "protection" or "defence" pull it the wrong way. We use AI too. We just use very little of it, and only to get the most out of the actual words.

The story is balance, not defence. Every clinician is getting an AI scribe. Who's writing things down for the patient?

Some lines to play with:

  • The AI surge is here. This is the gentle end of it.
  • Everyone's talking about AI. We're more interested in what was actually said.
  • Gentle AI, for the words that matter.
  • The AI does as little as possible. The words do the rest.

The voice

Plain, warm, a bit dry.

Write like a kind person explaining something to a neighbour who is tired and worried. Short sentences. No jargon.

Say

  • people
  • conversation
  • ask first
  • what was actually said
  • free to download, pay only for what you use
  • your record, on your phone

Avoid

  • users
  • protection, shield, safeguard
  • revolutionary, game-changing
  • specific launch dates
  • prices (we share them at launch)
  • anything that sounds like medical advice
Who decides what

Three tiers, so you can move fast.

I'm precious about a few things. The important thing is that it works, and I want you to go big. This is where the lines are, so we don't argue over every word.

Locked

These keep MyScribe safe to grow

Never sold. Never used to train AI. Audio isn't kept once the words are written down. Always ask first. No medicine names or doses in summaries. No claims that it's a medical device or gives clinical advice. The record belongs to the person. One wrong line here and a GP, a regulator or a journalist uses it against us just as we start to get noticed.

An idea I'd love you to run with

"This is where it's useful."

A YouTube series of short filmed scenes. Me to camera, then a real-life moment where MyScribe helps, then "after the conversation". It's tongue in cheek: the advert admits it's an advert, and then something true sneaks in under the joke. Like the product, it won't pretend.

Every scene opens with someone asking "Do you mind if I record this?", so the Standard is on screen without anyone reading it.

Mark, to camera, walking: Hi. This is MyScribe, and this is where it's useful.

Scene 1. Visiting Dad.

Mark: I'm off for my weekly visit to see Dad.

(beat)

He's not really my dad. He's an actor.

(beat)

He might be someone's dad, though. That might be the point.

Inside. Mark sits down, phone on the table.

Mark: Do you mind if I record this? Just to help me remember.

"Dad": Go on then.

A short, warm, rambling chat: the nurse he likes, a story from 1974, what he wants for his birthday.
